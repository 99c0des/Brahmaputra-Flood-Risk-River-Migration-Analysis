# Limitations and responsible interpretation

## 1. Screening, not prediction

The outputs are a historical GIS screening model. They do not forecast an exact future river channel, estimate a calibrated annual flood probability or establish that any individual home is unsafe. Relative risk classes must not be treated as absolute probabilities.

## 2. Different time windows

Historical river-water indicators span selected observations from 1986–2025. The flood-event-frequency input covers 2000–2018. These periods overlap but are not identical, so they represent different historical evidence.

## 3. Landsat water-mask uncertainty

Water detection depends on acquisition date, season, cloud/shadow masking, sediment, vegetation, mixed pixels, and the chosen NDWI threshold. A limited number of snapshots may miss short-lived water or seasonal channel changes. Water gain/loss is not automatically the same as bank erosion or permanent channel migration.

## 4. Coarse flood data

The Global Flood Database is MODIS-derived and commonly documented at approximately 250 m source scale. Reprojecting/resampling it onto a 30 m grid makes the rasters align for overlay; it does not produce true 30 m flood detail. Narrow channels, small settlements, and local flood variation may not be represented adequately.

## 5. Model choices

Normalization, weighting, NoData handling, raster alignment, thresholds and class breaks influence the final map. The classes are relative to this analysis and are not calibrated against observed damage or validated against an independent dataset.

## 6. Exposure is not vulnerability

Building footprints represent mapped structures. They do not tell us whether buildings are occupied, how many people live there, their age or mobility, building materials/condition, poverty, access to services, or ability to recover.

## 7. Missing risk dimensions

The project does not fully model:
- flood depth and flow velocity;
- event duration and warning lead time;
- rainfall/runoff and upstream dam operations;
- roads, evacuation routes, shelters, hospitals, schools, utilities, and other critical facilities;
- household and community vulnerability;
- land tenure, cultural ties, livelihoods, relocation feasibility, and community preferences;
- field-verified bank erosion or local engineering conditions.

## 8. Geographic scope

The working analysis polygon covers the project area around Majuli and nearby floodplain/river corridors. Results should not be generalized to all of Assam or to places outside the analysis boundary.

## 9. Responsible use

Use these maps to guide field verification, stakeholder discussion and collection of missing data. Any decision about protection, adaptation, restriction or relocation must involve qualified local authorities, local communities and additional hydrological, social, legal and environmental assessment. The map alone must not be used to justify displacement.
